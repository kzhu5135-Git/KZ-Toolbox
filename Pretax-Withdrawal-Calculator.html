Estimate only, not tax advice. Federal figures use 2026 amounts: standard deduction $32,200 (+$1,650 per spouse 65+), the $6,000-per-person senior deduction (phases out 6% above $150,000 MAGI), 2026 brackets, 0%/15%/20% qualified-dividend thresholds ($98,900 / $613,700 taxable income), 3.8% NIIT above $250,000 MAGI, and Social Security taxability tiers ($32,000 / $44,000). State taxes: Nevada has no personal income tax. Colorado is a flat 4.4% on federal taxable income (assumed to include the federal senior deduction), less Treasury interest, taxable Social Security for spouses 65+, and a pension/IRA subtraction of up to $24,000 per spouse 65+ that is shared with the Social Security subtraction; spouses under 65 get no subtraction here. Indiana is a flat 2.95% (2026) plus your county rate, after $1,000 personal exemptions per spouse plus $1,000 for each spouse 65+, with Social Security excluded. California has not published 2026 indexed amounts, so this uses the 2025 FTB brackets, standard deduction ($11,412), exemption credits ($153 each, $122 senior credit each) and the 1% surcharge above $1M; adjust with the inflation box. California does not tax Social Security. U.S. Treasury interest is taxed federally as ordinary income (and counts toward Social Security taxability and the investment income tax) but is exempt from state income tax. Bank interest is fully taxable at both the federal and state level. Long-term gains are taxed federally at the 0%/15%/20% rates (stacked on top of ordinary income) and as ordinary income by California and Indiana; enter the gain, not the sale proceeds. Ignores itemized deductions, short-term gains, loss carryforwards, state-tax SALT effects, tax-exempt interest, credits and AMT.
